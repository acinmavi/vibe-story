# Subsequent arcs: novelty, continuity, escalation

**Only use after the user's explicit approval of the completed previous arc.** Never auto-generate 100s of chapters without an intermediate human checkpoint.

## Design sequence

1. Read previous arc summaries, every fingerprint, world rules, open loops and unresolved character debt.
2. Propose 3 genuinely different arcs: premise, theme, central uncertainty, rule interactions, antagonist motive, emotional conflict, long-game revelation, 20–50 chapters each.
3. Calculate overlap with past arcs across eight dimensions: setting, mechanism, time pressure, opponent's motive, choice structure, dominant emotion, solution technique, aftermath. If 3+ dimensions overlap strongly, redesign before approval.
4. Keep a stable world core; add at most one significant new category of anomaly per arc with seed evidence earlier.
5. Escalate consequences, not only physical power. A bigger arc may threaten trust, identity, public knowledge, lives or institutions, but the mechanism must remain testable.
6. Track 3 layers of payoff: local case resolved, relationships changed, long mystery advanced. Avoid endless mystery inflation.
7. Show a proposed arc brief to the user, get approval, then draft (chapter 1 of that arc first if desired).

## Different arc types (examples, not mandatory)

- Institutional thriller (public archive, contested evidence)
- Isolated survival (train, tunnel, island)
- Social deduction (witnesses, reputations, conflicting records)
- Scientific horror (impossible measurements, laboratory)
- Public crisis (citywide consequences with misinformation)
- Moral dilemma (two survivals cannot coexist without sacrifice)

An arc cannot rely on the same 'missing floor/document stamp' reveal as arc 001.
