# Vietnamese prose — distinctive, readable serial fiction

Write originally in Vietnamese. Aim 1,900–3,000 Vietnamese whitespace-separated words per chapter (flexible at high-tension short chapters); avoid filler to satisfy count. Third-person limited following the current focal character except for clearly marked scene switches. Default present-day Vietnam-inspired fictional city; protect local detail and credible occupation.

## Scene discipline

- Each scene needs: immediate objective, obstacle, changed information/relationship, emotional temperature.
- Open with a concrete action or anomaly, not world-system exposition; embed explanation in choices.
- Mix sensory specifics with economical prose; characters notice things appropriate to their professions.
- In dialogue, give each person a distinct objective and cadence. Most lines should do at least two jobs (reveal character, escalate conflict, give or obscure evidence).
- Humor: dry contrast between professional routine and impossible events, occasional character reaction; not meme spam or quips every paragraph. Respect tragedy.
- End chapter with a genuine new choice, discovery or risk; not cheap 'but he did not know...' trick.
- No repeated recap of prior chapters. Assume readers remember major events; refresh only where needed.

## Vietnamese editorial watchlist

Reject pseudo-Chinese translation: 'bổn tọa', 'lông mày nhướng lên', 'hít một hơi khí lạnh', 'không khỏi', 'cười khổ' as automatic filler. Avoid formulaic AI constructions such as 'không phải X, mà là Y' in every scene, repeated 'bầu không khí đặc quánh', 'một cảm giác khó tả', theatrical second-person moralizations, monotonous one-line paragraphs.

Use Vietnamese idiom only when natural to speaker. Be precise about time, distance and measurements. Technical competence should emerge from observation and method, not piles of authoritative jargon. If an assertion would mislead about fire or building safety, call out uncertainty or consult reliable references.

## Revision passes

1. Mechanics pass: remove impossible knowledge and contradictions.
2. Character pass: motivation / subtext / independent choices.
3. Rhythm pass: sentence variety, scene transitions, tension-to-humor balance.
4. Natural-language pass: read out loud; trim canned phrases and over-narration.
5. Originality pass: compare overall features to inspiration; replace recognizably copied settings, names, exact jokes, signature scene patterns.
